Digital-arrest scams keep victims on a video call while their money moves. I4C has blocked over 59,000 WhatsApp accounts used for them — yet every reporting tool we found starts after the loss. cybercrime.gov.in answers panic with a checklist: 200 sanitized characters, a 12-digit UTR, OTP and CAPTCHA, before anyone helps.

Cyber Satark rebuilds the portal around the incident. The citizen speaks or types — Hindi, Marathi, English, switched mid-sentence. Deterministic rules sourced to MHA releases run before the model on every turn: if the story matches the digital-arrest pattern while the call is still live, the intake interrupts itself — end the call, no government agency investigates over video calls — spoken aloud in the citizen's language. An OpenAI GPT model extracts facts and asks only for what is missing; every fact waits for citizen confirmation; one confirmed record becomes the bank report, the 1930 call card, and the NCRP-mapped complaint. The learning corner runs the same rules in reverse — a talking scam-call simulator that teaches the three sourced tells. Tracking translates officialese: a hold is not a refund. Contact & Escalate becomes a case-aware escalation draft.

Honestly: the demo case is synthetic and labeled; filing and status are simulated — no public citizen API exists; AI suggests, rules route, the citizen decides. Codex read the NCRP citizen manual and produced the validation matrix the app runs on — every rule carrying its source, in the public repo.
