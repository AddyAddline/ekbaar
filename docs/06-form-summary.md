# Project summary for the submission form

Paste the text between the markers. Built the way the research says a skimmed
entry gets read: open on the failure they can picture, state the one idea,
then prove it with specifics, and disclose the mocks before anyone asks.
Every claim traces to docs/research/facts.md or the live portal audit in
docs/research/ncrp-audit.md.

---

"Just report it," people say. On cybercrime.gov.in today that means: diagnose your own crime, read a manual, then face OTP, CAPTCHA and a checklist. Incident details of 200 characters minimum, special characters banned. A 12-digit transaction number. An ID scan under 5 MB. Homework before help.

Cyber Satark rebuilds the portal around the incident instead of the form.

It asks your language first. Then you just talk: Hindi, Marathi, English, typed or spoken. Rules sourced to Ministry of Home Affairs releases run before the model every turn. If your story matches the digital-arrest pattern while the call is still live, the intake interrupts itself and says aloud, in your language: end the call, no government agency investigates over video calls. The form waits. The scam will not.

An OpenAI model extracts the facts. You confirm each one, and nothing unconfirmed is used. That record becomes a bank fraud report, a 1930 call script, and an NCRP complaint mapped to the live checklist. Track keeps official messages word for word and explains what each establishes, what it does not (a hold is not a refund), and your next step.

The learning corner is practice, not PDFs: take a scam call safely, or paste a suspicious message for a verdict from sourced facts only, which admits when it doesn't know.

The demo case is synthetic. Filing and status are simulated and labeled on screen. Codex browsed the real portal and its citizen manual, turning the government's own requirements into the rules this runs on.
